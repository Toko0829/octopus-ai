# Organic social for a founder-led product

## Treat it as distribution, not as brand presence

Maintaining a profile because a business is expected to have one produces
posting without purpose and costs more time than it returns. Organic social
earns its place when it is doing a specific job: putting the product's ideas in
front of people who do not know it exists.

If a channel is not doing that job, the honest options are to change how it is
used or to stop, not to keep posting at a lower standard.

## The founder has an advantage the brand account does not

Audiences engage with people more readily than with companies, and platforms
tend to reflect that. For a solo founder this is a structural advantage rather
than a preference: building in public, showing the reasoning behind decisions,
and posting from a personal account usually outperforms the same material
published by a logo.

It also carries a real cost worth naming. Attention accrues to the person, not
the asset, and it is not transferable if the founder later steps back.

## Native beats cross-posted

Every surface has its own norms for length, pacing, and what an opening looks
like. Publishing one asset identically everywhere reads as syndicated and
performs worse on all of them than a version rebuilt for each. The idea travels
between platforms; the execution should not.

## Consistency compounds, virality does not

A post that reaches far once produces a spike and little durable change. Regular
posting at a standard the audience can rely on produces a slower curve that
keeps rising. Planning around the possibility of going viral is planning around
an outcome that cannot be commissioned.

The practical implication is to choose a cadence sustainable in a bad week
rather than a good one.

## Replies are distribution

Conversation in comments and replies is typically weighted heavily by these
platforms, and it reaches the audiences of the people being replied to. Time
spent in other people's threads often produces more reach for a new account than
time spent producing another post of one's own.

## Follower count is the least useful number available

It measures accumulated history, not current reach, and it can grow while
influence falls. The signals worth tracking are how many people saw the material
who do not already follow, how many took the offered next step, and how many
moved to a channel you control.

## Move attention to something you own

Social audiences are rented. Reach depends on ranking decisions that change
without notice, and an account can lose distribution or be suspended without
recourse. The purpose of the channel is partly to convert borrowed attention
into an owned one, which in practice usually means an email list.

A post that earns wide reach and offers no next step converts none of it, and
the reach does not return.
