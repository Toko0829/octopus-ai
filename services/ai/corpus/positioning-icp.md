# Positioning and choosing who you are for

## Positioning is upstream of every channel

Positioning decides who the product is for and why they should care. Every
downstream activity inherits it: ads, content, landing pages, email. When
positioning is wrong, no channel tactic rescues it, and the failure is usually
misread as a channel problem. Teams respond by changing creative, then
audiences, then platforms, and the numbers stay flat because the message was
never aimed at anyone in particular.

## The diagnostic

Two patterns point at positioning rather than execution:

Ads earn clicks but not conversions. The hook is interesting enough to
interrupt someone, and the promise underneath does not match what they wanted.

Content earns traffic but not signups. The topic attracts readers who were never
going to buy, which usually means the content was chosen for reach rather than
for the problem the product solves.

In both cases more budget makes the loss larger, not smaller.

## Narrow converts better than broad

Broad positioning feels safer because it appears to keep every opportunity open.
In practice it competes with everyone and speaks specifically to no one. A
narrower statement of who the product is for reduces the addressable audience on
paper and raises conversion enough to more than compensate at early scale.

Narrowing is a claim about the problem, not a demographic filter. "For people
who already tried the manual approach and hit a wall" is a sharper boundary than
an age range or a job title, because it selects on a situation the reader can
recognise in themselves.

## Where the answer comes from

Positioning is discovered, not invented. The sources worth mining, in order:

People who already bought. What did they try before, and what pushed them to
switch on the day they switched?

People who churned. What did they expect that did not arrive?

People who considered and did not buy. This group is the hardest to reach and
the most informative, because they saw the pitch and disagreed with it.

Competitor reviews describing frustration are the cheapest substitute when no
customer base exists yet.

## Write it down as a testable sentence

State who it is for, the situation they are in, what changes for them, and what
they would otherwise do. Anything that would still read as true if a competitor
published it is not positioning, it is category description.

Then treat it as a hypothesis. Positioning is validated by whether strangers
convert, not by whether it sounds right internally.

## When to bring in a human

Positioning work benefits from someone who is not attached to the product.
Founders are too close to their own build to hear which parts are ordinary. A
strategist earns their fee here more reliably than anywhere else in the funnel,
because the decision affects every downstream cost.
