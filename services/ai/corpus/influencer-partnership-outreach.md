# Influencer and partnership outreach

## What a partnership actually buys

A partnership puts the product in front of an audience somebody else has already
earned, carried by a recommendation from the person that audience trusts. The
endorsement is the asset. Nothing else in the funnel produces it.

The trade is control. You brief a partner and they decide how to say it, which is
uncomfortable and is precisely the reason it works: an audience that wanted your
wording would have read your own page.

## Choose by audience fit, not follower count

A creator with two thousand subscribers who are exactly your buyers outperforms
one with two hundred thousand who are not, at a fraction of the cost. Reach among
the wrong people flatters a report and produces nothing.

Ask who is actually in this audience, whether they act on recommendations or only
watch, and whether anything adjacent has worked here before. Comments are more
informative than follower counts, because they show whether anybody is listening.
All of this depends on knowing who your buyers are; if that is vague, the work
belongs upstream in positioning rather than here.

## What to pay a creator, and the shapes a partnership deal takes

Four shapes cover almost every creator partnership: a flat fee for agreed
content, a commission on the sales or leads that result, free product with no
fee, or a hybrid of a modest fee plus commission that splits the risk.

Anchor the number to what the outcome is plausibly worth, not to follower count,
because follower count is what the other side will anchor to. Estimate the
signups, bookings or purchases one collaboration could realistically drive,
multiply by what one is worth to you, and let that set the ceiling. If a
collaboration plausibly drives twenty signups and a signup is worth five units,
the deal is worth about a hundred, whatever the audience size suggests.

Start smaller than feels ambitious. One paid test with a clear brief tells you
whether this partner converts; partners who convert can be scaled, and partners
who do not have cost one fee rather than a quarter's budget. Expect most to
produce very little. It is a portfolio, not a bet. Recurring arrangements with
the few who work outperform a constant search for new ones, because a second
mention from a trusted person converts better than the first.

## The outreach itself

Short, specific, and evidently written by somebody who has seen their work. Say
what you are, why their audience in particular, and what you propose, in that
order and in a few sentences. Ask for something small first.

Template blasts are deleted because they read as exactly what they are, and the
people worth working with get several a day. Expect a low reply rate even when
this is done well: it is a pipeline of leads like any other, and the arithmetic
works only if the list is long enough and the follow-up is polite, once.

## Running the collaboration

Give a brief, not a script. One page: the single message that must land, the
claims that must not be made, and the practical details. Everything else is
theirs, because a creator reading your copy aloud sounds like a script, which is
the one thing you are paying to avoid.

Give every partner their own link or code. Without it you cannot tell which
partnership produced the enquiries and installs, and you will renew whoever came
most recently to mind rather than whoever worked. Agree in advance whether you
may reuse the material yourself, and for how long.

## Disclosure is not optional

Paid and gifted partnerships are advertising, and the relationship must be
disclosed clearly wherever the recommendation appears. That is an obligation on
both sides rather than a courtesy, and a platform's own partnership label may not
be sufficient by itself. The specifics are covered separately; the rule here is
that it belongs in the brief rather than being discovered afterwards.

## When to bring in a human

Contracts carrying usage rights, exclusivity or a term of months are negotiation
and legal territory rather than marketing judgement, as is any partner dispute
once it has started. The outreach and the brief can be drafted for you. The
signature and the terms should not be.
