# Controlling cost per acquisition on paid social

## Start with a testing budget, not a scaling budget

An early campaign's job is to buy information, not customers. Set a fixed test
budget you are willing to lose entirely and treat it as research spend. Scaling
decisions come after you have a signal, not before.

Set a CPA ceiling before launch, derived from unit economics rather than from
what feels affordable. If a customer is worth 40 units of margin over the period
you are willing to wait for payback, a CPA above that is a loss you are choosing
to repeat. Write the ceiling down; it becomes the guardrail that pauses the
campaign automatically.

## Test hooks, not audiences, first

On platforms with strong automated targeting, creative is the lever that moves
performance most, and audience configuration matters less than it used to. Run
several distinct hooks against the same audience rather than the same creative
against several audiences. Distinct means a different claim or angle, not a
different colour of button.

Give each variant enough budget to exit the learning phase. Splitting a small
budget across many variants produces many inconclusive results rather than one
clear answer.

## Kill losers early, scale winners slowly

Underperforming variants should be paused on a schedule you set in advance, so
the decision is not made emotionally while watching a dashboard. Increase budget
on winners in modest steps; large jumps reset the platform's optimisation and
typically degrade the performance you were trying to buy more of.

## Attribution will disagree with itself

Platform-reported conversions and your own analytics will not match, because
they attribute differently and platforms count conversions they influenced.
Pick one source as the number of record for decisions, usually your own, and
use platform numbers only for relative comparison between variants inside the
same platform.

## When to bring in a human

Sustained underperformance after several creative iterations is usually a
positioning problem rather than an ads problem, and no amount of budget
reallocation fixes it. That is the point to route the work to a strategist
rather than continuing to iterate creative.
