- **perf(ai): a planning turn can time itself, and the two things that measurement then found.** Every latency number in `ai-orchestrator.md` had been taken by hand, because the service contained exactly one `time.monotonic()` and it was in the rate limiter. `retrieval.py` now times embed, hybrid search and rerank per pass; `retrieval complete` carries `embed_ms`/`search_ms`/`rerank_ms`, `decomposed retrieval` adds `fanout`, `passes`, `base_ms`, `subqueries_ms`, summed `rerank_ms` and `total_ms`, and `local rerank pass` carries `padding_ratio`, `tokenize_ms` and `forward_ms`.   **The measurement contradicted the plan it came from, in both directions.** `RERANK_FANOUT` runs at most K sub-query passes at once under an `asyncio.Semaphore` while `runtime.thread_budget` sets torch to `TORCH_NUM_THREADS // K`, so demand is divided rather than multiplied. That was expected to be roughly a wash, the suspected escape being a large fixed per-pass cost implied by a recorded 34.0s-against-36.8s when candidates were halved. **Both halves were wrong**: the fixed cost is **1.4s, 4% of a pass**, and the pass is essentially linear in candidates at 1.28s each. Fan-out wins anyway, because **thread scaling is sublinear past 8** — halving a pass's threads costs about **21%** more CPU time, not 100%, so two passes sharing the budget beat two taking turns with all of it. **And the larger win was the one that was not the headline:** `padding=True` padded all 25 pairs to the longest, so one long chunk was paid for 25 times; length-sorted batching took padding from **37.4% of every token computed to 11.3%**. Measured over 6 sub-queries at 16 threads: 25 candidates went **209.3s at fanout 1 with no batching to 116.9s at `RERANK_FANOUT=2` / `RERANK_BATCH_SIZE=8`**, a 1.79x improvement on top of the 1.86x the thread budget already bought. Fanout 3 was marginally faster and was refused: `16 // 3` is 5, so it idles a core, and the 6% gap sits inside the run-to-run spread the benchmark showed on identical settings.

  **Nothing may move a score, and nothing did.** Batching cannot, because the attention mask already excludes padding; max drift across the whole twelve-row grid was **1.9e-07**. The five golden negatives were re-run through the real pipeline at both the defaults and the compose values and came back on **identical digits**, including the 0.001007 that is the lower bound of the 1.76x threshold margin — the narrowest safety property in retrieval ([ADR-0009](docs/40-adr/0009-local-reranker.md)). So the 65-minute eval gate was not spent: retrieval results did not change. **Then driven through the container for real**, on the goal that produced the recorded 267s: `total_ms=121007` over 6 sub-queries, with `subqueries_ms=92507` against a summed `rerank_ms=185866` — the gap between those two is the concurrency, read off a log line rather than inferred. Int8 quantisation, the one lever likely to halve a pass, is explicitly **not** in this slice for the opposite reason: it does move scores, and would need the gate plus a threshold recalibration.

  **Two defects fixed on the way, both found by running the thing rather than reading it.** `Providers` built the `LocalReranker` on an unguarded `is None` check, so two coroutines arriving together each loaded ~1 GB of weights; that needed two concurrent requests to surface under the sequential loop and would have been routine under fan-out, so it is behind a lock with the unlocked fast path kept so tests can still inject a fake. And the reranker was **never warmed at startup** while the embedder has been since ADR-0008, so the first plan on every fresh process paid **18.4s** of cold load inside the request against roughly 2s warm; it now warms in the lifespan with a real forward pass, not a bare load, because the first pass initialises kernels that loading does not. **Warming removes the stall, not the peak**, and that distinction was a correction made by measuring rather than a claim written first: the weights are resident before `/health` goes green (4.0 GiB, at **37s** against a 180s `start-period`), while the peak still arrives with the first real pass because what grows there is activations. The fan-out raises that peak from **5.3 to 5.9 GiB**, which is the one thing `RERANK_FANOUT=2` costs and the reason to lower it, rather than the thread count, on a small instance.

- **feat(agent): the AI executor and a deterministic checker, so an approved task actually finishes.** Tasks were stopping at `ROUTING` with `awaiting_executor`, which was the honest thing to report and also the visible end of the chain. Now an AI-owned step runs `ROUTING → AI_RUNNING → AI_SELF_CHECK → APPROVED`, producing a cited artifact, and **an approved task satisfies its dependents**, which is what makes the graph move at all. **The maker** is a new `/execute` on `services/ai`, and it **re-retrieves for the step rather than inheriting the plan's sources**: the plan was retrieved for a whole goal, and the sources that justified "you need positioning work" are broader than the ones that help write the positioning, so reusing them hands the model the goal's context to answer a much narrower question, which is the mismatch decomposition exists to fix one level up. It runs through the **same groundedness gate as planning**, applied where it matters more, because by then a person has approved the plan and ungrounded output stops looking like a suggestion and starts looking like delivered work. Refusing is a correct outcome and **escalates rather than retrying**, since asking the same core about the same corpus produces the same refusal more slowly. A third proposal kind, `write_artifact`, whose citations are source **labels** rather than indices: the checker has to catch a source the maker was never given, and an index is checkable for range but not for provenance. Citations naming an unsupplied source are **dropped in the core rather than passed on**, because the checker escalates a fabricated one and turning a model slip into a human's problem is worse than letting the remaining grounding be judged on its merits. **The checker is deterministic, and that is a decision rather than a placeholder.** `business-projects-workflow.md` says "AI critic" and an LLM judge is the obvious reading; this project already rejected that shape for the generation eval on the grounds that a judge bills per call and returns a different answer each time. The argument is stronger here, because this verdict decides whether a task **unblocks its dependents**, so a flaky one propagates through the graph. So it checks what is checkable, `empty_output`, `too_short`, `lost_grounding` (rule 10 applied to the work: a step the plan cited whose output cites nothing has quietly become ungrounded while the card still shows it as cited) and `fabricated_citation`, and refuses to pretend about the rest. An LLM critic belongs **on top of** this later, not instead of it. **`fabricated_citation` never retries**, because asking the same maker again is how you get a second invented source; everything else gets one bounded re-do, since "produced nothing" is the failure a retry plausibly fixes. **Each attempt is its own `task_runs` row**, so a retry never erases why the previous one failed, and the retry loop lives in the executor rather than in the scheduler because the scheduler only ever selects `PENDING` tasks and would never revisit this one. - **feat(core): the scheduler and the router, and the first Node-side tests.** `packages/core` finally exists, holding the two decisions this phase turns on: which tasks may run, and who is allowed to run them. **The router is the point.** `owner_type` already carries what the planner proposed (a plan step's AI / HUMAN / YOU), and a router that simply believed it would be putting authorisation back in the prompt, which is exactly what rules 7 and 11 forbid. So the first rule **outranks `owner_type` entirely**: a `high_risk` task goes to `needs_user` however the plan labelled it, because a planner marking an irreversible step `AI` is the case this exists to catch. The fourth rule applies **rule 10 to work rather than to prose**: an AI-owned step with no citations escalates instead of running, since letting it proceed would make the citation requirement cosmetic, with the claim flagged unverified while the action it gates goes ahead anyway. `read_only` is exempt, deliberately, because research that changes nothing gates nothing and escalating it would bury the marketplace in zero-risk work, which is how a safety rule gets switched off. - **feat(db): the task DAG and its state machine, enforced in Postgres.** The first Phase 2 step, and deliberately not the durable runner.
